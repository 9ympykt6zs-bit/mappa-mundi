# Regional Demonstration Model

## Purpose

This document defines the proposed product philosophy for recognizing that a learner knows a geographic region well enough to receive regional credit in Mappa Mundi.

It distinguishes decided principles, likely design directions, and unresolved questions. It does not describe functionality that is already built and does not prescribe a final assessment, scoring formula, evidence schema, learner-state model, menu, or access rule implementation.

## Decided principle: open regional access

Mappa Mundi must allow learners to study any available region at any time. Regional progress must never become a prerequisite chain across the world.

A high school student preparing for a test on European geography should be able to begin studying Europe immediately, even if they have never studied or demonstrated knowledge of the United States. Likewise, a learner's familiarity with one continent should not determine whether they have permission to enter another.

Four related concepts must remain distinct:

### Access

**Open.**

A learner may enter any available geographic region regardless of progress elsewhere. Demonstration status, lesson completion, scheduler state, and progress in another region do not determine access.

### Recommended progression

**Guidance.**

Mappa Mundi may recommend preparation, a learning sequence, or a useful next region. Recommendations can explain why one experience may be easier or more valuable after another, but they should not prevent access.

### Regional progress

**Evidence of what the learner has practiced and demonstrated.**

Regional progress may describe ongoing learning, attempts, strengths, needs, or other evidence within that region. It is not permission to enter or leave the region.

### Demonstrated regional knowledge

**Optional recognition of broad competence.**

Demonstrated regional knowledge recognizes that a learner has convincingly shown integrated understanding of a region. It is a high-bar distinction, not a required step in a global progression chain.

Demonstration is never required to:

- leave a region;
- begin another region;
- access another part of the world.

## Working definition

> **Demonstrated regional knowledge is a voluntary, high-bar, non-gating assessment of broad integrated geographic understanding, requiring strong overall performance, competence across major domains, and successful higher-order spatial reasoning.**

This is a working definition. It establishes the product intent while leaving the exact qualification model open to testing, educational review, and calibration.

No final numeric threshold follows from this document.

## Likely assessment direction: integrated understanding

A regional demonstration should assess integrated geographic understanding rather than isolated trivia or narrow activity completion.

For a mature regional experience such as the United States, likely domains include the following.

### Political map

- place locations;
- place identification and naming;
- capitals;
- important political relationships.

### Physical geography

- major rivers;
- lakes;
- mountain ranges;
- coasts;
- other important physical systems.

### Spatial structure

- adjacency;
- relative position;
- regional reconstruction.

### Geographic reasoning

- routes;
- relationship questions;
- multi-step spatial reasoning;
- other challenges that require a mental model rather than recognition alone.

The exact domains and their relative importance may differ by region. A region should be assessed according to meaningful geographic structure, not forced into a U.S.-specific content checklist. The common requirement is breadth: the learner should demonstrate knowledge across the major kinds of understanding that define the region.

A learner should not earn regional credit merely by performing extremely well in one domain while showing a major gap in another. Strong capital recall, for example, should not compensate completely for an inability to locate places or reason about the regional map. A future qualification model may therefore combine strong overall performance with minimum competence in each major domain.

This document does not set those thresholds.

## Higher-order reasoning

Higher-order spatial tasks are especially valuable because they require the learner to use an internal mental model rather than recognize one isolated answer.

Consider the distinction:

> Where is Arizona?

This primarily tests location recognition.

Now consider:

> Create a route from Arizona to North Carolina using adjacent states.

This may test:

- adjacency;
- directional reasoning;
- regional structure;
- mental-map construction.

Route construction requires the learner to coordinate several relationships across a larger spatial model. Reconstruction likewise requires the learner to understand relative position and how multiple places fit together. These tasks can provide stronger evidence of integrated geographic understanding than a sequence composed entirely of multiple-choice recall.

A regional demonstration should therefore include some higher-order geographic reasoning. Recognition and naming remain important foundations, but they should not be the only forms of evidence used to recognize broad regional competence.

## High bar, but not perfection

The intended standard is convincing evidence, not flawless performance.

- Earning **Demonstrated** should be harder than ordinary lesson completion.
- The result should reflect broad and current competence rather than participation alone.
- Occasional mistakes should not automatically prevent recognition of strong regional understanding.
- A major gap in an essential domain should matter even when overall performance is otherwise strong.
- Exact overall and domain-level thresholds should be calibrated later.

A future model might combine:

- a high overall performance threshold;
- minimum competence within each major domain.

That is a likely direction, not a finalized scoring rule. Calibration should consider learner age, assessment reliability, content breadth, question difficulty, regional differences, and the consequences of false positive or false negative recognition.

## Hints and assistance

A regional demonstration is intended to show what the learner can do independently. The conditions of the attempt therefore matter to what the result can establish.

Mappa Mundi may still allow support so that an unsuccessful or difficult attempt remains useful for learning. Support should not feel punitive, and a learner should not be trapped in an assessment after discovering that they need instruction. However, substantial assistance should weaken the evidence for earning Demonstrated status.

The governing distinction is:

- **independent retrieval** — strong demonstration evidence;
- **assisted retrieval** — useful learning evidence, but potentially insufficient for Demonstrated status;
- **guided exposure** — teaching rather than demonstration.

This document does not define which hints a demonstration may offer, how assisted outcomes affect qualification, or whether an attempt can transition from assessment into teaching. Those decisions should follow the broader [Learning Support and Hint Philosophy](learning-support-and-hints.md) while preserving the purpose of regional demonstration.

## Relationship to learning progress

Learning progress and regional demonstration are related, but they are not identical.

### Learning progress

Accumulated evidence from ordinary use of Mappa Mundi. It may reflect instruction, practice, review, independent retrieval, assisted work, exposure, errors, and change over time.

### Regional demonstration

A focused attempt to establish that the learner can currently perform across the region's major knowledge domains under conditions appropriate for recognition.

The product should not equate:

- time spent;
- activity completion;
- internal scheduler mastery;
- regional Demonstrated status.

A learner may complete many activities without yet providing broad demonstration evidence. Another learner may arrive with strong prior knowledge and demonstrate competence without completing Mappa Mundi's recommended lessons. Internal mastery heuristics may inform recommendations or readiness, but they are not automatically the same claim as a voluntary regional demonstration.

Demonstration should also avoid fabricating a lesson history. Recognizing prior knowledge means recording what the learner showed, not pretending that they completed learning experiences they bypassed.

## Prior-knowledge pathway

The model should eventually support two learner-chosen pathways into a region.

### Learn

Follow the recommended regional progression through instruction, practice, feedback, and increasingly integrated challenges.

### Demonstrate

Attempt an integrated challenge designed to show existing knowledge across the region's major domains.

If the learner succeeds:

- mark the region as Demonstrated;
- give appropriate regional credit;
- allow the learner to continue exploring normally;
- keep regional learning experiences available for optional review or deeper study.

If the learner does not succeed:

- do not restrict regional or global access;
- use the result, where appropriate, to recommend useful learning areas;
- allow the learner to continue studying in that region or anywhere else;
- allow a future retry under a policy that remains to be designed.

An unsuccessful demonstration is information, not a penalty. It can help the learner identify what to strengthen without converting regional access into an examination gate.

## Global implications

The model should generalize beyond the United States to regions such as Central America, Europe, South America, and future world regions.

Regional demonstration should be independent. A learner might eventually have a profile such as:

- **United States — Demonstrated**
- **Central America — Learning**
- **Europe — Demonstrated**
- **South America — Not started**

This profile describes knowledge state, not permission to enter those regions. No row is a prerequisite for another.

The content needed to demonstrate a region will vary. A compact region with a bounded country-location curriculum cannot be assumed to have the same demonstration length or domain structure as a mature United States experience. The reusable principle is broad, integrated evidence appropriate to the region—not identical item counts or a copied U.S. assessment.

## Product guardrails

The following are decided guardrails for future design work:

- Keep regional access open regardless of progress or demonstration elsewhere.
- Treat demonstration as voluntary and non-gating.
- Assess breadth across meaningful domains rather than rewarding one narrow strength.
- Include higher-order spatial reasoning when the regional content can support it.
- Require convincing evidence without demanding perfection.
- Preserve the distinction between independent, assisted, and guided performance.
- Do not infer Demonstrated status from time spent or activity completion alone.
- Do not convert internal mastery estimates automatically into learner-facing regional claims.
- Do not fabricate completion of bypassed lessons.
- Keep unsuccessful attempts useful for learning and recommendation rather than punitive.

## Open questions

- What exact overall threshold should qualify as Demonstrated?
- What minimum competence should be required within each domain?
- How long should a regional demonstration take?
- Should demonstration challenges adapt to the learner?
- How should assisted answers affect qualification?
- When should a learner be allowed to retry?
- Should Demonstrated status ever decay or require later reconfirmation?
- How should regional Demonstrated status appear in Progress Report?
- How should demonstration scale for regions with very different amounts of content?
- Which domains are universal, and which should be defined separately for each region?
- How should the product explain a result that is strong overall but below the minimum in one domain?
- Should normal accumulated evidence ever be sufficient for Demonstrated status, or should the status always require a focused attempt?

## Design status

The open-access and non-gating principles in this document are intended product guardrails. The working definition and assessment domains are proposed directions. Qualification thresholds, assessment composition, adaptivity, retry policy, decay, learner-facing terminology, evidence representation, and Progress Report treatment remain unresolved.
